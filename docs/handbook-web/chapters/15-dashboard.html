Part 6 · Around the Meeting

Your dashboard

Every time you sign in to Canvas M and you are not inside a meeting, the dashboard takes over the whole screen. It is the home base where your meetings, your projects and your calendar all live side by side. This chapter walks every button, panel and badge you will meet there.

The lay of the land
  1. 1 Brand & top bar — the Canvas M wordmark on the left; the action cluster on the right (notifications, history, wallpaper, theme, language, Use the canvas solo, and your account chip).
  2. 2 Left sidebar — your views: My meetings and Invited / Upcoming, plus the list of Projects you belong to.
  3. 3 Middle column — the meeting cards for whatever you picked on the left. A header shows the context name, a Grid/List toggle, a Sort by menu and status filter chips.
  4. 4 Right calendar — a month calendar that always stays visible, tinted to match your meeting colours. Drag its left edge to make it wider or narrower.
  5. 5 Resume banner — appears above everything when you left a live meeting open and came back; one click drops you straight back in.

Notifications, history, and your account

The top bar carries the controls that belong to you, not to any single meeting. From left to right it reads as four clusters: a row of round icon buttons, the language capsule, the Use the canvas solo button, and finally your account chip.

The icon buttons are, in order: the bell (pending invitations, covered below), the history clock (My activity), the image tile (wallpaper), and the moon (light / dark theme). The history panel is a reverse-chronological log of what you did — it lists Created meeting, Invited to, Joined meeting and Uploaded to My Files rows, with an Accepted or Declined chip next to invitations you answered. When you have done nothing yet it simply reads No activity yet.

The clock is your past; the bell is your to-do list. They are deliberately kept apart.
Wallpaper

The wallpaper picker dresses the desk behind the glass panels. Pick any preset tile, or choose Tải ảnh lên (Upload image) to use your own photo — it applies instantly and is remembered on this browser. The small x on your uploaded tile returns you to the default. Wallpaper is purely cosmetic and only you see it.

Switching what the middle column shows

  1. Open My meetings to see your whole personal calendar of meetings — everything you organize or attend, across every project.
  2. Open Invited / Upcoming to see only the meetings you were invited to. This view never shows an edit pencil — you do not edit a meeting you were merely invited to.
  3. Scroll to the Projects heading to see the projects you belong to. Each row may show a colour dot and the project's stage; a project you were only invited into carries an Invited badge instead.
  4. Click any project to list just its meetings. The project name becomes the column title.

→ The middle column reloads for whatever you picked, and any open detail or form is cleared so you never see a stale view.

Note

If the project list can't load, the empty slot says Couldn't load — check your network connection. with a Retry link, rather than pretending you have no projects.

Host-only

Internal staff also see My Files, Guest manager and Manage projects entries here, plus a New project button at the bottom. As a participant these are hidden — projects are created and run for you.

Reading and opening a card

  1. Each card shows the meeting's title (optional emoji icon and topic), its date and time, the creator (avatar + name), a coloured status pill, and — in the overview and invited lists — a clickable project chip.
  2. Click the body of a card to open the meeting. A live or scheduled one joins you; a finished one opens read-only for review.
  3. Click the project chip on a card to jump straight to that project's list instead of opening the meeting.
  4. Use Grid / List in the header to switch the layout, and Sort by to order by By time, By title or By status.
  5. Use the status chips — All, Live, Upcoming, Done, Cancelled — to narrow the list to one lifecycle stage.

→ When sorted By time the list groups by day with Today / Tomorrow / date separators — upcoming days first (nearest first), then past days (most recent first).

Note

Two small icon buttons on every card — a Palette and a Details (eye) — let you tint the card or open its full detail panel. If the cards can't load you'll see Couldn't load — check your network connection. with Retry; an empty list reads No meetings in this project yet.

The colours a card can wear

A green Live pill and a pulsing live dot. Clicking the card joins the meeting in progress.

An Upcoming card carries a Scheduled pill and its planned date and time. Joining goes through the start gate until the host begins.

A Finished or Cancelled pill marks a closed meeting. Opening it shows the canvas read-only — you can review and extract, but not change anything.

Glance at the pill before you click: green means walk straight in, a date means it has not started, and a grey pill means review-only.

Invitations that need your answer

The bell collects every meeting invitation still waiting on your reply, so none slip past. A small numbered badge shows how many are pending; click it to open the Notifications & invitations panel.

Each row shows the meeting title, when it is (or a red LIVE NOW tag if it is already running), and the project name. Two buttons sit on the right: Accept and Decline. Accepting a meeting that is live counts as "I'm coming" and joins you on the spot; accepting a scheduled one simply confirms your seat. Declining removes the row and quietly confirms with Invitation declined. When nothing is pending the panel reads No notifications.

Good to know

An invitation grants access but sends no email or push — the bell is the notification channel, refreshed automatically about once a minute. The bell only ever lists things you can still act on; finished and cancelled meetings drop off by themselves.

The Resume banner

If you closed the tab or refreshed while a meeting was still going, the dashboard greets you with a Resume meeting banner above the columns, showing the meeting's title. One click takes you straight back in.

The banner is careful: it only appears for a meeting that is genuinely resumable. A meeting that has since been ended for everyone, or cancelled, never offers Resume — the banner re-checks the meeting's real status when you return to the tab, and quietly removes itself if the meeting is over. A scheduled meeting that has not started yet is not offered either; you join those through the normal start gate.

Use the canvas solo

Separately, the Use the canvas solo button in the top bar drops you onto a blank private canvas with no meeting and nobody else — handy for a quick sketch. Leaving it returns you to the dashboard.

Note

These four are the only canonical statuses. Older meetings may carry legacy labels, but Canvas M maps them onto this set so the pill is always one of these four.

Host-only

Starting a scheduled meeting, ending one for everyone, cancelling and restoring are organizer / project-authority actions. As a participant you only ever join, review, or answer an invitation.

Dashboard troubleshooting

The middle column shows "Couldn't load" — The server didn't respond when fetching your meetings or projects

Click Retry; check your internet connection if it keeps failing → 15.4

A card won't open — The room couldn't be reached or you don't have access

You'll see "Couldn't open the meeting…"; confirm you were invited and try again → 15.5

An invitation isn't in the bell — It's already finished, cancelled, or you already answered or joined

The bell only lists open, unanswered invitations; check the card in your lists instead → 15.7

The Resume banner disappeared — The host ended or cancelled the meeting while you were away

Expected — the banner re-checks status on focus and drops dead meetings → 15.8

No "New meeting" or project tools — Those are organizer / internal-staff controls

Ask a host to schedule the meeting and invite you → 15.4

Note

Most dashboard errors are transient network hiccups — a Retry usually clears them. If sign-in itself fails, that's covered earlier in the login chapter.
