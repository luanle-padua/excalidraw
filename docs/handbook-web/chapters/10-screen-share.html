Screen sharing

One presenter at a time, broadcast live to the whole room. Share your screen with a single click, or watch someone else's in a floating viewer you can pop onto a second monitor. This chapter covers presenting, watching, audio, and every state the button can be in.

One screen, shared to everyone

Screen sharing in Canvas M is deliberately simple: at any moment one person in the room can present their screen, and everyone else watches it live. There is no "request to present" queue and no host approval step — whoever clicks Present first holds the floor until they stop.

Your screen travels over a separate live-media channel, not the drawing canvas. That means the canvas, chat, and captions keep working normally while a share is running. As a viewer you don't have to do anything to start watching — the moment a presenter begins, a floating viewer appears for you automatically. When the presenter stops (or leaves the room), the viewer disappears on its own.

Whoever clicks Present first holds the floor. There is no queue and no host approval.
Presenter → live channel → every viewer The presenter's screen is sent over a dedicated media channel. Viewers join silently — no camera or microphone prompt — purely to receive the screen.
Single-share lock

Because only one person can present at a time, your Present button is disabled while someone else is presenting. Wait for them to finish, then it unlocks.

Share your screen

  1. Find the Present button in the meeting header — it sits in the media group beside the microphone and camera controls, drawn as a monitor-with-arrow icon. Hover it to confirm the Present tooltip.
  2. Click Present. Your browser opens its own picker asking which entire screen, window, or browser tab you want to share.
  3. Choose a source and confirm in the browser dialog. Sharing begins the instant the real screen feed starts — not when you click, so cancelling the picker leaves nothing shared.
  4. Watch the Present button take on its active (highlighted) state. That is your confirmation you are now the presenter; you keep seeing your own screen, so no extra viewer window opens for you.
  5. When you're done, click the highlighted Present button again to stop. The share ends immediately and everyone's viewer closes.

→ You are presenting. Every other participant sees your screen in a floating viewer, and a small screen badge appears on your tile in the participants bar.

Note

Cancelling the browser's screen picker is harmless — nothing is broadcast and the button returns to its normal state. Presence is only announced once the live feed actually starts.

Host-only

There is no host "give me the screen" or "stop their share" control. Sharing is self-service and governed only by the single-share lock — a host who wants to present simply waits for the current presenter to stop.

Every state of the Present button

Default. The monitor icon with the Present tooltip. Click to open the screen picker.

Highlighted/active. You are the current presenter. Click again to stop sharing.

Disabled. Another participant is presenting (single-share lock). It re-enables the moment they stop.

Disabled, with the tip "Screen sharing isn't supported on this device or browser (e.g. iPad / iPhone). Present from a computer instead."

Disabled. You're reviewing a finished meeting — live sharing is switched off entirely in read-only review.

While you present, a 📺 badge labelled "Sharing screen" sits on your avatar in the participants bar.

If the button looks greyed out, check these states in order: are you in a finished-meeting review, is someone else already presenting, or is your device simply unable to share?

When someone else presents

  1. Do nothing — the floating viewer appears automatically the moment a presenter starts. Its header reads "{name} is presenting" with a live indicator dot.
  2. Use the — (minimize) button in the viewer header to collapse it to just the title bar when you need the canvas, then ▢ (Expand) to bring the picture back.
  3. If your browser supports it, use the ⧉ button (Pop out) to float the share in a separate, draggable window — handy for a second monitor (see the next section).
  4. Live captions, when on, pin to the bottom edge of the viewer so you can follow along with the spoken audio.
  5. When the presenter stops or leaves, the viewer closes by itself. If they only minimised their shared window, the picture pauses and recovers automatically when it reappears — you don't need to do anything.

→ You see the presenter's live screen in a movable viewer, with captions and any shared audio carried along with it.

Note

You join the share silently to watch — Canvas M never prompts for your camera or microphone just to receive someone's screen.

Pop the share into its own window

The ⧉ button — tooltip "Open in a separate window to drag to another screen" — lifts the live screen out of the in-app viewer and into a standalone, always-on-top window you can drag onto a second monitor. The drawing canvas keeps the rest of your first screen to yourself.

While popped out, the in-app viewer shows the note "Watching in a separate window" in place of the picture, and live captions travel with the share into the pop-out window. Closing the pop-out window — or clicking back in the app — returns the picture to the in-app viewer and brings the captions home. The viewer also re-homes itself automatically if the presenter stops while you're popped out.

In-app viewer → pop-out window on monitor 2 Pop out moves the live video into its own OS window without re-loading the feed, so the picture never flickers as it leaves the app.
Browser support

Pop out uses the browser's Document Picture-in-Picture feature, which today is Chromium-only (Chrome, Edge, and similar). On Firefox and Safari the ⧉ button simply doesn't appear, and the in-app viewer remains your way to watch.

Hearing a shared tab or system sound

If a presenter chose to include audio when picking a tab or screen — for example to play a video clip — Canvas M plays that shared sound to you automatically alongside the picture.

Browsers sometimes block audio from starting until you've interacted with the page. If a share's sound doesn't come through at first, simply click anywhere in the app (or press any key); the audio resumes on that first gesture. Shared audio is separate from the meeting's voice call — muting yourself or others in the call has no effect on a presenter's shared tab sound.

Presenter tip

Sharing system or tab audio is a choice you make in your browser's screen-picker dialog (e.g. a "Share tab audio" checkbox), not in Canvas M. If viewers can't hear your clip, re-share and tick that box.

Host-only

None of these are host controls. There is no moderator screen-share lock, force-stop, or approval step — the single-share lock is fully automatic.

When sharing misbehaves

Present button is greyed out — Someone else is already presenting (single-share lock), you're in a finished-meeting review, or your device can't share.

Wait for the current presenter to stop, exit review mode, or present from a computer if the tip says it's unsupported. → 10.4

Tooltip says screen sharing isn't supported — Your browser has no screen-capture API — typically iPad / iPhone Safari.

Present from a desktop or laptop browser instead, as the tip advises. → 10.4

Toast: "Couldn't present your screen." — The share failed to start — connection or media error (any detail appears after the message).

Click Present again to retry; check your network if it persists. → 10.3

Clicked Present but nothing shared — You cancelled the browser's screen-picker dialog. Sharing only begins when a source is confirmed.

Click Present again and pick a screen, window, or tab. → 10.3

Viewer froze or went black — The presenter minimised the shared window, so its picture paused.

No action needed — it recovers automatically when they bring the window back. → 10.5

No sound from a shared clip — The browser blocked audio until you interact, or the presenter didn't share audio.

Click anywhere in the app to resume audio; ask the presenter to re-share with tab audio on. → 10.7

No Pop out button in the viewer — Your browser doesn't support Document Picture-in-Picture (Firefox / Safari).

Use the in-app viewer; minimise it to free up the canvas. → 10.6

Joined mid-share and see nothing — A late joiner is reconciled against the live share automatically.

The viewer should appear within moments; refresh the page if it doesn't. → 10.5

Note

The error toast and the disabled-button tip are the only on-screen messages screen sharing surfaces; everything else recovers silently.
