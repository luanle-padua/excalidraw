Part 7 · Run the Room

Hosting the meeting

From the moment you press Start meeting to the moment you press End meeting, you hold the room. This chapter walks the host's powers in order — starting a scheduled meeting, inviting the right people, letting guests in from the waiting room, moderating who speaks and who stays, and ending cleanly so an AI recap is waiting for everyone afterwards. Every label here is the one you will see on screen.

A meeting moves through four states — and only actions move it

A meeting is never edited into a new state; it is acted into one. Canvas M tracks exactly four canonical statuses — Scheduled, Live, Finished and Cancelled — and each transition has its own button. There is no status dropdown anywhere in the editor, by design.

Scheduled → Live happens when someone with start rights presses Start meeting. Live → Finished happens when a host presses End meeting (the red power button). Scheduled → Cancelled happens from the meeting's detail panel via Cancel meeting. The one way back out of cancelled is Restore meeting, which returns it to Scheduled; a cancelled meeting can also be disposed of permanently with Delete forever. Finished is immutable — you cannot edit, reschedule, or reopen it as live again; reopening a finished room lands everyone in read-only review.

Finished is the one-way door. End the meeting only when you mean it.
Status flow: Scheduled ↔ Cancelled, Scheduled → Live → Finished The server enforces the same transitions the buttons offer — there is no other path between states.
Who can manage

Lifecycle controls appear for the organizer, the meeting host, a designated co-host, or a project authority (division head / project leader). Everyone else sees the meeting but none of the management buttons — the worker re-checks the same rule, so a hidden button is also a blocked request.

Starting a scheduled meeting

  1. Open the meeting link or pick the meeting from your project. If it is still Scheduled, the full-screen start gate appears with the meeting title and, when set, Scheduled for ….
  2. If you belong to the owning department, you see a Start meeting button. Press it. While it works the label reads Starting….
  3. If you are not allowed to start (a cross-department invitee), you instead see a spinner and Waiting for the host to start the meeting… — the gate polls every five seconds and drops you straight into the room the instant any host starts it.
  4. Once live, you land in the meeting shell: canvas in the centre, the header across the top, the people strip along the bottom.

→ The meeting flips to Live in the registry, which is what releases every other person still waiting on the gate. Your own join is identical to any other live join.

Host-only

The Start meeting button is gated by the server-computed owning-department scope (organizer / host / co-host / project authority / same-division member). A guest or outside invitee never sees it — they wait. If the meeting was cancelled or finished while you were parked on the gate, pressing Start re-reads the truth and routes you to the cancelled notice or read-only review instead of barging in.

The host's console
  1. 1 Project · meeting title — click to edit name / topic / metadata (organizer-only; a ⌄ chevron shows when editable). Tooltip: Click to edit meeting name / topic.
  2. 2 Meeting clock — an objective timer counting from when the host started, so late joiners see the same elapsed time. Tooltip: Meeting time elapsed.
  3. 3 People count — reads joined / invited; click to open the Participants panel. A small mic sub-badge shows how many are in the audio call.
  4. 4 Media group — mic, camera and leave-call (from the call controls) framed with Present (screen share). Present locks while someone else is sharing.
  5. 5 Captions (CC) and Transcript — toggle live captions from any view; the transcript icon carries a count badge of logged segments.
  6. 6 Meeting group — layout switcher, Invite (accent, organizer/host/co-host only), and Projects (host-only folder).
  7. 7 End meeting — the red Power button at the far right. Host / co-host / organizer / authority only. Ends the room for everyone.

Inviting colleagues and project guests

  1. In the header's Meeting group, press the accent Invite button (a person-plus icon). The Invite people panel slides in.
  2. Pick Internal colleagues from the staff directory — type in Search by name / department… and click a name to add a chip. Each pick shows their title and division.
  3. Add this project's issued guests from the second list (search field placeholder below the Briefcase label). Guests must already be issued in the project — there is no free-typed external email here, by design (per-department confidentiality).
  4. When at least one internal person is selected, the Also grant project access (internal only) checkbox appears (on by default) — leave it on to give them whole-folder access, not just this one meeting.
  5. Press Grant access (the button shows a live count, e.g. Grant access (3)). On success it reads Access granted ✓ — remember to send guests the link and copies the room link to your clipboard.

→ Invitees get an access grant (a login plus a meeting_invitee row). There are no automatic invite emails — Canvas M deliberately does not share by raw room URL, so paste the copied link to the people you just granted. Use Copy invite link at the top of the panel any time to grab it again.

Host-only

Invite is visible only to the organizer, host, co-host, or project authority, and only while the meeting still takes edits — it disappears in a finished meeting and in read-only review. If the worker refuses, the panel shows the exact reason: You don't have permission to invite people to this meeting. (403) or The meeting has ended or was cancelled — no more invites. (409).

Letting guests in — admit or deny

  1. External guests who join a live meeting they haven't been admitted to are parked in a waiting room — they see Waiting to be let in and Waiting for the host to let you in…. Internal staff auto-admit and never appear here.
  2. On your side, open the Participants panel (click the people count). When guests are knocking, the people strip shows an N waiting badge and the panel grows a Waiting to join (N) section.
  3. For each knocker, press Admit (a check icon) to let them in, or Deny (red) to refuse. The row drops immediately; a failed call re-fetches so a knock is never silently lost.
  4. A denied guest sees Not admitted with The host hasn't let you in right now. You can knock again in a moment. and a Knock again button — the re-knock is rate-limited to once every 30 seconds, server-side.

→ Admitted guests connect immediately, entering muted (the room starts with audio off). If you end the meeting while a guest is mid-admit, their client re-reads the meeting and quietly backs out rather than barging into a finished room.

Host-only

The knock list polls every five seconds and is gated on server-confirmed authority, not the local acting-host election — so only the organizer / host / co-host / project authority ever sees people waiting. A plain internal participant, even one the room briefly elected as acting host, sees no waiting section.

Who can mute, remove, and end

The host (and a project authority) sees per-person Mute / Unmute and Remove on every other tile — both as hover buttons on the avatar strip and as full buttons in the Participants panel. The mute button reads the real broadcast state, so it offers Unmute when the person is already muted. A removed person sees The host removed you from the meeting.

A designated co-host carries a half-crown Co-host badge and can End meeting for everyone. In-room kick/mute follow the acting-host rule; the co-host role specifically authorises ending and inviting, which a random participant cannot do.

A project-scoped guest is never a moderator. Even if the socket host-election momentarily lands on them, mute / remove / end stay hidden — and the worker also refuses the host command server-side. Guests keep leave, transcript, present, and their own profile.

Reach for moderation when a line is open and feeding back (mute), when someone joined the wrong room (remove), or to hand the ending to a co-host. The crown badge over an avatar always tells the room who is host without anyone having to ask.

Ending the meeting for everyone

  1. Press the red End meeting (Power) button at the far right of the header. Confirm the prompt: End the meeting for EVERYONE? All participants switch to read-only review.
  2. Canvas M marks the meeting Finished in the registry first. If that write fails it aborts and toasts Couldn't end the meeting — check your network and try again. — nothing changes, so you can simply retry.
  3. On success, an AI recap is generated in the background from the transcript and chat (never blocking the ending) and stored on the meeting as its AI summary.
  4. Everyone in the room is broadcast into read-only review, your own resume pointer is cleared so the meeting is never offered for resume again, and your own view switches to review.

→ The meeting is now Finished and immutable. Reopening it lands in review showing the banner Reviewing (read-only) · meeting finished — extract only. The auto-recap appears as a clamped AI summary card in the meeting's detail panel, with Show more / Show less for long recaps.

Host-only

End is gated by designated role, not the socket election: only the host, co-host, organizer, or a division head / project leader sees the button. If nothing was said or typed, no recap is generated — the summary card simply stays absent. Generation is fire-and-forget, so a model hiccup never delays the meeting ending.

Reschedule, cancel, restore, delete

Reschedule Reschedule → Save new time Status is Scheduled and you can manage it Reveals date / time (30-min slots) / duration fields; saving writes the new schedule. Save is disabled until a date is chosen. Once a meeting is Live the time can no longer change (Meeting has started — date & time can no longer change.).

Note

Open these from the meeting's detail panel: select the meeting in your project to load its hero card, where the lifecycle buttons sit beneath the schedule line.

Host-only

All five actions are organizer / authority gated and disabled while a save is in flight. A refused save surfaces Save failed — check your permission (organizer only) or the meeting state.

Host troubleshooting

No Start meeting button on the gate — You are not in the owning department — only organizer / host / co-host / authority / same-division members may start.

Wait for the spinner Waiting for the host to start the meeting…; the gate auto-joins you the instant a host starts. Or ask the organizer to start. → 22.3

Invite button is missing from the header — You are a plain participant or guest, or the meeting has finished / is in read-only review.

Inviting needs organizer / host / co-host / authority on a still-editable meeting. A finished meeting can't be invited into. → 22.5

Guest stuck — no Waiting to join section appears for you — The knock poll runs only for server-confirmed authority; an acting-host who isn't the organizer doesn't see knocks.

Have the organizer, host, co-host or division head open the Participants panel to admit. Guests can press Knock again after the 30s cooldown. → 22.6

Mute / Remove not on a participant's tile — You aren't the host / authority, or the tile is yourself or the host (you can't moderate those).

Moderation appears only on OTHER participants and only for the host or a project authority. → 22.7

Ending the meeting toasts a failure — The registry write to mark it Finished didn't go through (network).

Couldn't end the meeting — check your network and try again. Nothing changed — press End meeting again. → 22.8

No AI summary card after a meeting — Nothing was said or typed, so there was no transcript or chat to recap; or the summariser hiccuped (non-blocking).

Expected when a meeting had no audio or chat. Recaps are best-effort and never block ending — the card simply stays absent. → 22.8

Can't change the date — fields say it's locked — The meeting is already Live; only Scheduled meetings can be rescheduled.

Meeting has started — date & time can no longer change. Reschedule before starting, or end and create a new one. → 22.9

Note

Most hidden host controls are also blocked at the server, so a missing button is a real permission boundary, not a UI glitch — check your role first.
